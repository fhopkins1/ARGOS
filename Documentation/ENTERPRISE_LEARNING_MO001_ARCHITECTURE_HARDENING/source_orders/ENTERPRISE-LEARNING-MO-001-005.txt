# ENTERPRISE-LEARNING-MO-001-005

## Learning Product Architecture Challenge

### Objective

Challenge the constitutional architecture governing every learning product created, validated, published, restricted, superseded, retired, or withdrawn by the Enterprise Learning Office.

This order shall determine whether each permitted learning product represents constitutionally appropriate enterprise knowledge without assuming authority over truth, decisions, workflow execution, risk acceptance, performance certification, monitoring, or historical custody.

The review shall simplify the learning-product architecture wherever complexity does not preserve a necessary constitutional guarantee.

---

## Review Authority

This challenge shall derive authority from:

* ARGOS Constitution;
* Enterprise Constitutional Standard ECS-003;
* Enterprise Constitutional Standard ECS-004;
* Enterprise Truth Doctrine;
* Enterprise Evidence Doctrine;
* Enterprise Audit Doctrine;
* Enterprise Reproducibility Doctrine;
* Workflow Ownership Doctrine;
* Historian Constitutional Baseline;
* Performance Truth Constitutional Baseline;
* ENTERPRISE-LEARNING-RM-001;
* ENTERPRISE-LEARNING-MO-001.

This order shall not create new learning-product authority.

---

## Challenge Scope

The review shall challenge the constitutional design governing:

* hypotheses;
* predictive models;
* causal models;
* anomaly models;
* explainability products;
* uncertainty products;
* model evaluation artifacts;
* feature definitions;
* learning-derived recommendations;
* publication records;
* reproducibility declarations.

The review shall determine whether each product class is:

* constitutionally necessary;
* uniquely defined;
* non-duplicative;
* independently owned;
* appropriately governed;
* deterministically published;
* scientifically supportable;
* constitutionally bounded.

---

## Product-Class Necessity Challenge

For every learning-product class, determine:

* why the class must exist;
* which constitutional purpose it serves;
* whether another class already serves that purpose;
* whether it introduces distinct ownership or lifecycle requirements;
* whether it creates unnecessary abstraction;
* whether it can be represented as evidence, metadata, or a subtype of another product.

A product class shall be retained only where its separate existence preserves a material constitutional distinction.

Classes that differ only by implementation technique shall be consolidated.

---

## Canonical Product Taxonomy Challenge

Challenge whether the learning-product taxonomy can be reduced to a smaller set of canonical constitutional classes.

Evaluate whether the architecture should distinguish only among:

* hypothesis products;
* empirical findings;
* predictive products;
* causal products;
* anomaly products;
* uncertainty representations;
* explanation artifacts.

Determine whether:

* feature definitions are independent products or governed dependencies;
* model evaluations are products or required evidence;
* recommendations are products or advisory projections of another product;
* reproducibility declarations are products or mandatory publication attributes;
* publication records are products or lifecycle evidence.

The final taxonomy shall minimize classes without collapsing constitutionally material differences.

---

## Hypothesis Product Challenge

Challenge whether the hypothesis architecture clearly distinguishes:

* proposed relationships;
* testable propositions;
* supported hypotheses;
* rejected hypotheses;
* unresolved hypotheses;
* competing hypotheses.

Verify that hypotheses cannot be represented as:

* enterprise truth;
* validated model conclusions;
* operational recommendations;
* decision authority;
* causal fact without sufficient evidence.

Determine whether separate lifecycle states are necessary or whether status may be represented through a smaller deterministic classification model.

---

## Predictive Product Challenge

Challenge the constitutional architecture governing predictive models and predictions.

Determine whether the architecture clearly distinguishes:

* model definition;
* trained model artifact;
* model version;
* prediction execution;
* prediction output;
* evaluation evidence;
* publication status.

Verify that a predictive product cannot constitutionally:

* select an enterprise action;
* create a workflow decision;
* authorize execution;
* determine risk acceptability;
* certify future truth;
* conceal prediction uncertainty.

Determine whether prediction outputs should be governed as transient executions of a published model rather than independent long-lived products.

---

## Causal Product Challenge

Challenge the constitutional design governing causal models and causal findings.

Verify that the architecture distinguishes:

* causal hypothesis;
* identification strategy;
* causal model;
* causal estimate;
* causal interpretation;
* supporting evidence;
* residual uncertainty.

Determine whether the current architecture adequately prevents:

* correlation from being presented as causation;
* model assumptions from being concealed;
* causal claims from exceeding observed scope;
* causal findings from becoming enterprise mandates;
* causal publications from being treated as historical truth.

Require explicit constitutional treatment of confounding, identification assumptions, falsifiability, and alternative causal explanations.

---

## Anomaly Product Challenge

Challenge whether anomaly models and anomaly findings are constitutionally separated.

Determine whether the architecture distinguishes:

* anomaly detector definition;
* baseline definition;
* threshold definition;
* anomaly score;
* anomaly event;
* anomaly interpretation;
* escalation recommendation.

Verify that an anomaly product cannot independently:

* declare a fault;
* declare fraud;
* establish operational truth;
* suspend execution;
* alter workflow state;
* direct intervention.

Determine whether anomaly findings should remain observations requiring interpretation by a constitutionally authorized office.

---

## Explainability Product Challenge

Challenge whether explainability should exist as:

* an independent published product;
* a required component of every learning product;
* a linked evidence artifact;
* a consumer-specific presentation layer.

Determine whether separate explainability products create:

* duplicated ownership;
* version inconsistency;
* explanation drift;
* publication-state mismatch;
* excessive lifecycle complexity.

Every published learning product shall possess sufficient explanation, but the architecture shall avoid unnecessary duplication between the product and its explanation.

---

## Uncertainty Product Challenge

Challenge whether uncertainty is properly modeled as:

* an independent product;
* an inseparable property of another learning product;
* a reusable uncertainty model;
* a publication requirement;
* a runtime output.

Determine which uncertainty forms require separate constitutional identity.

The architecture shall distinguish, where material:

* epistemic uncertainty;
* aleatoric uncertainty;
* model uncertainty;
* data uncertainty;
* parameter uncertainty;
* distributional uncertainty;
* unresolved scientific uncertainty.

Uncertainty shall not be separable from the learning conclusion where separation could permit false precision.

---

## Recommendation Architecture Challenge

Challenge whether learning-derived recommendations should exist as a separate constitutional product class.

Determine whether a recommendation:

* merely translates a learning product into advisory language;
* creates hidden decision authority;
* duplicates Analyst responsibility;
* encroaches upon Commander authority;
* creates execution coupling;
* obscures the underlying evidence and uncertainty.

Where recommendations are permitted, they shall:

* remain explicitly advisory;
* identify the authorized decision owner;
* preserve the underlying learning product;
* disclose assumptions and uncertainty;
* prohibit direct execution;
* avoid consuming the workflow baton.

If these safeguards cannot be maintained, recommendation products shall be removed from Enterprise Learning authority.

---

## Product Identity Challenge

Challenge whether the required product identity model is minimal and complete.

Determine whether every learning product requires:

* unique identifier;
* product class;
* version;
* lifecycle state;
* owner;
* evidence reference;
* dataset reference;
* feature reference;
* experiment reference;
* validation reference;
* provenance reference;
* publication authority;
* reproducibility status.

Remove identity fields that are purely implementational.

Retain every field required to establish ownership, meaning, lineage, use, or certification.

---

## Product Composition Challenge

Determine which information shall be intrinsic to the learning product and which shall remain linked evidence.

Challenge duplication of:

* datasets;
* feature definitions;
* experiment records;
* evaluation results;
* assumptions;
* uncertainty estimates;
* explainability artifacts;
* provenance records;
* publication evidence.

A learning product shall contain sufficient information to establish meaning and permitted use without duplicating authoritative records owned elsewhere.

---

## Ownership Challenge

Verify that Enterprise Learning owns only:

* learning-product definition;
* learning-product scientific content;
* learning-product validation requirements;
* learning-product publication status;
* learning-product restrictions;
* learning-product retirement;
* learning-product supersession;
* declared limitations;
* declared reproducibility status.

Challenge any claimed ownership over:

* source historical records;
* historical provenance;
* enterprise truth;
* workflow decisions;
* risk determinations;
* execution authorizations;
* trade instructions;
* certified performance;
* monitoring observations owned by Monitoring.

Ownership shall be reduced wherever another constitutional office possesses superior authority.

---

## Publication Authority Challenge

Challenge whether publication authority is:

* explicitly assigned;
* independent from model development;
* evidence-dependent;
* deterministic;
* limited to Enterprise Learning products;
* separated from operational authorization.

Determine whether publication requires multiple approval layers or whether a single constitutionally accountable publication authority is sufficient.

Eliminate ceremonial or duplicative approvals.

Retain independent review only where it protects a distinct constitutional interest.

---

## Publication-State Challenge

Challenge every proposed publication state, including:

* Proposed;
* Experimental;
* Under Validation;
* Validated;
* Approved for Publication;
* Published;
* Restricted;
* Suspended;
* Superseded;
* Retired;
* Withdrawn;
* Rejected.

For each state, determine:

* whether it produces a distinct legal effect;
* whether it changes permitted use;
* whether it changes ownership;
* whether it changes consumer access;
* whether it changes historical treatment;
* whether it can be represented as metadata instead.

Merge states that do not produce materially different constitutional consequences.

No lifecycle shall preserve states solely because they are convenient to implementation.

---

## Consumer Contract Challenge

Challenge whether every published product requires an explicit consumer contract defining:

* intended consumers;
* permitted uses;
* prohibited uses;
* required inputs;
* output semantics;
* uncertainty semantics;
* freshness requirements;
* scope limitations;
* failure conditions;
* escalation path.

Determine whether this contract should be standardized across product classes.

Consumer-specific exceptions shall be minimized.

No product shall imply authority through undocumented consumer interpretation.

---

## Truth-Boundary Challenge

Verify that no learning product becomes enterprise truth solely because it is:

* statistically significant;
* highly accurate;
* replicated;
* peer reviewed;
* reproducible;
* validated;
* published;
* operationally useful.

Challenge all terminology that could cause a learning conclusion to be interpreted as certified truth.

Learning products shall use constitutionally precise classifications such as:

* estimated;
* inferred;
* predicted;
* modeled;
* observed;
* supported;
* unsupported;
* uncertain.

Truth certification shall remain outside Enterprise Learning authority.

---

## Decision-Boundary Challenge

Verify that no learning product architecture permits Enterprise Learning to:

* choose an enterprise action;
* rank actions as binding;
* consume the decision baton;
* direct a workflow owner;
* authorize risk;
* direct the Trader;
* trigger broker execution;
* replace Commander or Analyst judgment.

Challenge automated consumption interfaces that could silently convert advisory knowledge into an operational command.

Any operational consumer shall require independent constitutional authority to act upon a learning product.

---

## Performance-Boundary Challenge

Verify separation between:

* model evaluation;
* learning-product quality;
* operational outcome measurement;
* certified enterprise performance.

Enterprise Learning may evaluate model behavior.

It shall not certify enterprise performance.

Challenge any metric, report, or model score that could be mistaken for a Performance Truth Record.

---

## Historical-Boundary Challenge

Verify that Enterprise Learning may create operational lineage but cannot own immutable historical provenance.

Challenge whether the architecture correctly separates:

* active learning dependencies;
* scientific lineage;
* model lineage;
* publication lineage;
* immutable historical provenance.

The Historian shall remain the exclusive owner of historical custody and preserved provenance.

Enterprise Learning shall not overwrite prior product versions or historical relationships.

---

## Scientific Sufficiency Challenge

Determine whether every published product class requires adequate scientific governance.

Challenge whether publication requirements sufficiently address:

* hypothesis falsifiability;
* dataset suitability;
* feature validity;
* experimental design;
* validation independence;
* calibration;
* uncertainty;
* sensitivity;
* alternative explanations;
* out-of-sample behavior;
* reproducibility;
* known failure conditions.

Requirements shall be proportional to the product’s scope, risk, and intended use.

---

## Explainability Sufficiency Challenge

Determine whether explainability requirements are:

* necessary;
* proportionate;
* consumer-appropriate;
* independently verifiable;
* resistant to misleading simplification.

Challenge explainability mechanisms that merely describe implementation without explaining product behavior, evidence, assumptions, uncertainty, and limitations.

Require stronger explanation where the product materially affects enterprise decisions.

---

## Reproducibility Challenge

Verify that every published product declares whether it is:

* fully reproducible;
* conditionally reproducible;
* partially reproducible;
* not reproducible;
* not yet evaluated for reproducibility.

Challenge publication of products lacking reproducibility status.

Determine whether non-reproducible products may be published only under explicit restriction.

Reproducibility status shall not be inferred from successful execution alone.

---

## Product Versioning Challenge

Challenge whether every change requires a new product version.

Distinguish among:

* material scientific change;
* dataset change;
* feature change;
* model change;
* validation change;
* uncertainty change;
* explanation change;
* documentation-only change;
* presentation-only change.

Only changes affecting product meaning, validity, scope, or permitted use shall require constitutional version advancement.

Immaterial changes shall not create unnecessary product proliferation.

---

## Supersession Challenge

Challenge whether supersession is the correct mechanism when:

* a better model exists;
* assumptions change;
* source data changes;
* validation fails;
* scope changes;
* reproducibility is lost;
* scientific understanding changes.

Determine whether supersession, suspension, withdrawal, and retirement are sufficiently distinct.

Each retained mechanism shall produce a unique constitutional consequence.

---

## Restriction and Withdrawal Challenge

Verify that restricted, suspended, retired, withdrawn, rejected, and superseded products cannot be consumed as active general-purpose products.

Challenge whether access controls, retrieval semantics, and consumer warnings are constitutionally sufficient.

Products presenting material risk of misleading use shall fail closed at retrieval.

---

## Failure-Behavior Challenge

Challenge product behavior when:

* identity is unresolved;
* version is ambiguous;
* evidence is missing;
* provenance is unavailable;
* required inputs are absent;
* inputs violate scope;
* freshness is exceeded;
* validation is incomplete;
* uncertainty cannot be calculated;
* reproducibility status is invalid;
* publication status is inactive;
* consumer use is prohibited.

The architecture shall prohibit authoritative-looking outputs under invalid conditions.

---

## Duplication Analysis

Identify duplication between:

* hypotheses and causal products;
* model evaluations and evidence;
* uncertainty products and product attributes;
* explainability products and publication requirements;
* recommendations and Analyst outputs;
* anomaly findings and Monitoring observations;
* reproducibility declarations and certification records;
* publication records and lifecycle evidence.

Every duplication shall be eliminated, justified, or assigned an explicit constitutional purpose.

---

## Hidden Authority Analysis

Identify product structures that could conceal:

* truth authority;
* decision authority;
* execution authority;
* risk authority;
* performance authority;
* historical authority;
* workflow ownership.

Terms such as “recommended action,” “approved model,” “certified prediction,” “validated truth,” and “required response” shall be challenged for constitutional ambiguity.

---

## Simplification Determination

For each challenged product class or requirement, assign one disposition:

* Retain;
* Retain with Clarification;
* Consolidate;
* Reclassify as Evidence;
* Reclassify as Product Attribute;
* Reassign Ownership;
* Remove;
* Redesign.

Every retained element shall have a unique constitutional purpose.

## Acceptance Criteria

This order shall be considered complete only when:

* every retained learning-product class serves a unique constitutional purpose;
* unnecessary product classes are consolidated or removed;
* model definitions, executions, outputs, evidence, and publications are clearly distinguished;
* uncertainty and explainability cannot be separated from products in a manner that creates misleading use;
* hypotheses cannot be represented as truth;
* predictive products cannot become decisions;
* causal products cannot become enterprise mandates;
* anomaly products cannot independently trigger intervention;
* recommendation products cannot conceal decision authority;
* publication authority is explicit and minimal;
* publication states are reduced to those producing distinct constitutional consequences;
* all products possess deterministic identity, scope, ownership, evidence, uncertainty, explanation, and reproducibility requirements;
* historical provenance remains exclusively owned by the Historian;
* model evaluation remains separate from Performance Truth;
* invalid, stale, unsupported, ambiguous, or unauthorized products fail closed;
* the resulting architecture represents the simplest constitutionally complete system for publishing enterprise learning knowledge.
