# ENTERPRISE-LEARNING-RM-001-005

## Learning Product Architecture

### Objective

Define the constitutional architecture governing every learning product created, validated, published, superseded, retired, or withdrawn by the Enterprise Learning Office.

This order shall establish the permitted classes, required contents, ownership boundaries, lifecycle controls, publication rules, and constitutional limitations governing Enterprise Learning outputs.

Enterprise Learning shall publish knowledge products for enterprise consumption.

It shall not publish enterprise decisions, truth determinations, execution commands, authorizations, performance certifications, or historical conclusions.

---

## Constitutional Product Classes

The Enterprise Learning Office may constitutionally create and publish:

* hypotheses;
* predictive models;
* causal models;
* anomaly models;
* uncertainty estimates;
* explainability artifacts;
* feature definitions;
* model evaluation artifacts;
* learning-derived recommendations;
* research findings;
* learning limitations;
* reproducibility declarations.

No other learning product class shall be published without constitutional authorization.

---

## Hypothesis Architecture

A hypothesis shall represent a testable proposition concerning an enterprise-observable relationship, behavior, condition, or outcome.

Every hypothesis shall include:

* immutable hypothesis identifier;
* stated proposition;
* intended scope;
* originating question;
* supporting observations;
* assumptions;
* falsification criteria;
* required evidence;
* known limitations;
* status;
* responsible owner;
* lifecycle state.

A hypothesis shall not be represented as established truth.

Unvalidated hypotheses shall remain explicitly classified as unvalidated.

---

## Predictive Model Architecture

A predictive model shall estimate a future, unknown, or unobserved outcome from declared inputs.

Every predictive model shall include:

* immutable model identifier;
* prediction target;
* input feature definitions;
* training dataset identity;
* validation dataset identity;
* modeling method;
* training procedure;
* evaluation metrics;
* confidence characterization;
* uncertainty characterization;
* scope of applicability;
* known failure conditions;
* reproducibility status;
* publication state;
* version identity.

Predictive output shall remain advisory unless consumed by an authorized decision-owning workflow.

The model shall not own the resulting decision.

---

## Causal Model Architecture

A causal model shall represent a declared causal hypothesis concerning relationships among enterprise variables or events.

Every causal model shall identify:

* causal question;
* treatment or initiating condition;
* outcome definition;
* causal assumptions;
* confounders;
* adjustment method;
* identification strategy;
* supporting evidence;
* uncertainty;
* limitations;
* falsification conditions;
* validation status.

Correlation shall not be represented as causation without explicit causal justification.

Causal conclusions shall remain learning products and shall not become historical truth by publication alone.

---

## Anomaly Model Architecture

An anomaly model shall identify observations, behaviors, sequences, or conditions that deviate from a defined reference state.

Every anomaly model shall define:

* monitored domain;
* reference population or baseline;
* anomaly criteria;
* scoring method;
* detection threshold;
* expected false-positive behavior;
* expected false-negative behavior;
* uncertainty;
* scope limitations;
* escalation interface;
* publication status.

An anomaly finding shall not independently authorize intervention, execution, or workflow alteration.

---

## Uncertainty Estimate Architecture

Every material learning product shall include an uncertainty representation appropriate to its product class.

Uncertainty estimates may include:

* confidence intervals;
* prediction intervals;
* probability distributions;
* calibration measures;
* sensitivity ranges;
* scenario ranges;
* epistemic uncertainty;
* aleatoric uncertainty;
* data sufficiency limitations;
* unresolved model risk.

Uncertainty shall not be omitted where its absence could create false precision.

Unknown uncertainty shall be declared rather than concealed.

---

## Explainability Artifact Architecture

Explainability artifacts shall communicate how a learning product was produced, what evidence supports it, and where its conclusions may fail.

Explainability artifacts shall include, where applicable:

* supporting evidence;
* governing assumptions;
* feature contributions;
* model behavior summaries;
* influential observations;
* counterfactual explanations;
* sensitivity analysis;
* confidence;
* uncertainty;
* known limitations;
* reproducibility status.

Explainability shall not be satisfied by implementation documentation alone.

The explanation shall be interpretable by the constitutionally authorized consumer of the learning product.

---

## Learning Product Identity

Every learning product shall possess:

* globally unique identifier;
* product class;
* version;
* Enterprise Learning owner;
* creation timestamp;
* lifecycle state;
* originating dataset references;
* feature-definition references;
* experiment references;
* validation references;
* publication status;
* supersession status;
* provenance reference.

Identity shall remain stable across publication, retrieval, retirement, and historical preservation.

---

## Product Ownership

The Enterprise Learning Office shall own:

* learning-product definition;
* learning-product content;
* learning-product lifecycle;
* validation requirements;
* publication decision;
* retirement decision;
* supersession decision;
* product limitations;
* reproducibility declaration.

Enterprise Learning shall not own:

* historical provenance;
* enterprise truth;
* workflow decisions;
* execution authority;
* risk acceptance;
* trade authorization;
* performance certification;
* historical custody.

Historical provenance and immutable preservation shall remain owned by the Historian.

---

## Publication Architecture

A learning product may be published only when:

* its identity is established;
* its product class is valid;
* required evidence exists;
* validation is complete;
* assumptions are documented;
* uncertainty is characterized;
* limitations are declared;
* explainability requirements are satisfied;
* reproducibility status is recorded;
* publication authority is confirmed;
* provenance registration is complete.

Publication shall be an explicit lifecycle transition.

Draft, experimental, rejected, retired, and superseded products shall not be represented as active published products.

---

## Publication States

The constitutional publication states shall include:

* Proposed;
* Experimental;
* Under Validation;
* Validated;
* Approved for Publication;
* Published;
* Restricted;
* Suspended;
* Superseded;
* Retired;
* Withdrawn;
* Rejected.

Every state transition shall be deterministic, authorized, evidenced, and historically preserved.

No implementation-specific state shall bypass the constitutional lifecycle.

---

## Consumer Interface Contract

Every published learning product shall declare:

* intended consumers;
* permitted uses;
* prohibited uses;
* required interpretation;
* expected input contract;
* output contract;
* uncertainty semantics;
* freshness requirements;
* dependency requirements;
* failure behavior;
* escalation path.

Consumers shall not infer authority not explicitly granted by the product contract.

---

## Decision Separation

Enterprise Learning may provide:

* predictions;
* explanations;
* hypotheses;
* causal assessments;
* anomaly indications;
* ranked alternatives;
* uncertainty estimates;
* evidence summaries.

Enterprise Learning shall not:

* select the enterprise action;
* authorize workflow execution;
* approve risk;
* direct the Trader;
* certify enterprise performance;
* declare historical truth;
* alter historical records;
* consume the decision-owning workflow baton.

Any recommendation shall remain advisory and shall identify the constitutionally authorized decision owner.

---

## Truth Separation

A published learning product shall not become enterprise truth solely because it is:

* statistically significant;
* highly accurate;
* reproducible;
* validated;
* published;
* repeatedly observed;
* operationally useful.

Truth ownership shall remain with the constitutionally authorized truth-owning office.

Enterprise Learning may estimate, infer, predict, explain, or hypothesize.

It shall not constitutionally declare truth outside its own learning-product state.

---

## Historical Separation

The Enterprise Learning Office shall provide the Historian with:

* product identity;
* product version;
* supporting evidence references;
* dataset references;
* experiment references;
* model artifacts;
* publication events;
* retirement events;
* supersession events;
* reproducibility declarations.

The Historian shall retain exclusive authority over immutable historical custody and historical provenance.

Enterprise Learning shall not rewrite or delete prior learning-product history.

---

## Product Versioning

Every material change to a learning product shall create a new version.

Version changes shall preserve:

* predecessor identity;
* successor identity;
* change rationale;
* changed assumptions;
* changed data;
* changed features;
* changed method;
* changed validation;
* changed uncertainty;
* changed limitations;
* publication decision;
* supersession relationship.

Material changes shall not overwrite published versions.

---

## Product Supersession

A learning product may be superseded when:

* a validated successor exists;
* material assumptions change;
* data validity changes;
* feature definitions change;
* performance materially degrades;
* scope changes;
* reproducibility is lost;
* constitutional requirements change.

Supersession shall not erase the predecessor.

The successor shall explicitly identify the product it supersedes.

---

## Product Retirement and Withdrawal

Retirement shall occur when a product is no longer intended for active use.

Withdrawal shall occur when continued reliance would be constitutionally unsafe or materially misleading.

Retirement or withdrawal shall record:

* authority;
* reason;
* effective time;
* affected consumers;
* successor, if any;
* residual risks;
* required notifications;
* historical preservation status.

A retired or withdrawn product shall remain historically retrievable.

---

## Restricted Learning Products

A product may be restricted where:

* evidence is incomplete;
* validation is limited;
* applicable scope is narrow;
* uncertainty is elevated;
* data access is constrained;
* publication presents enterprise risk;
* experimental use remains constitutionally permissible.

Restrictions shall be explicit, machine-readable, and enforced at retrieval.

Restricted publication shall not be treated as general publication.

---

## Failure and Degradation Behavior

A learning product shall fail closed when:

* required inputs are absent;
* input definitions are violated;
* product version is unresolved;
* provenance is incomplete;
* reproducibility status is invalid;
* freshness requirements are violated;
* uncertainty cannot be represented;
* validation status is insufficient;
* the requested use exceeds published scope;
* the product is retired, withdrawn, or suspended.

Failure shall not silently produce an authoritative-looking output.

---

## Required Learning Product Record

Every published learning product shall contain or reference:

* product identity;
* product class;
* owner;
* version;
* lifecycle state;
* intended purpose;
* permitted use;
* prohibited use;
* input definition;
* output definition;
* dataset lineage;
* feature lineage;
* experiment lineage;
* evaluation results;
* assumptions;
* confidence;
* uncertainty;
* explainability artifact;
* limitations;
* reproducibility status;
* publication authority;
* historical provenance reference.

Incomplete products shall not enter the Published state.

---

## Constitutional Verification

Verify that the architecture prevents Enterprise Learning from:

* converting hypotheses into truth without authorization;
* converting predictions into decisions;
* converting anomalies into execution commands;
* converting causal assessments into enterprise mandates;
* concealing uncertainty;
* publishing unexplained models;
* overwriting prior product versions;
* modifying historical provenance;
* bypassing validation;
* publishing without lifecycle authority.

Each prohibited condition shall generate a constitutional failure.

---

## Acceptance Criteria

This order shall be considered complete only when:

* every permitted learning-product class is constitutionally defined;
* every learning product possesses stable identity, ownership, lifecycle, evidence, uncertainty, explainability, and provenance requirements;
* publication is governed by explicit deterministic state transitions;
* published products communicate permitted and prohibited uses;
* hypotheses remain distinguishable from validated findings;
* predictions remain distinguishable from enterprise decisions;
* causal models remain distinguishable from established truth;
* anomaly findings cannot independently authorize action;
* historical provenance remains exclusively owned by the Historian;
* product retirement, withdrawal, versioning, and supersession preserve immutable history;
* invalid, incomplete, stale, unsupported, or unauthorized products fail closed;
* Enterprise Learning publishes knowledge while all enterprise decision, execution, truth, risk, performance, and historical authorities remain constitutionally separate.
