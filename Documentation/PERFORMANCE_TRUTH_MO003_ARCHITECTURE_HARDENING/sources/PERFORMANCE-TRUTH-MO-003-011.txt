# PERFORMANCE-TRUTH-MO-003-011

# Enterprise Architecture Consistency Review

## Doctrine Statement

### Purpose

This Modification Order establishes the comparative constitutional review of the Performance Truth Office against every other certified ARGOS office.

The purpose of this order is to determine whether the Performance Truth Office applies enterprise constitutional doctrine consistently with the broader ARGOS architecture, including ownership, lifecycle, interfaces, evidence, fail-closed behavior, auditability, and certification.

The Performance Truth Office shall not be presumed architecturally correct merely because its internal doctrine is complete.

Any inconsistency between Performance Truth and another certified office shall be investigated to determine whether:

* Performance Truth is defective;
* the compared office is defective;
* enterprise doctrine is incomplete;
* both offices rely upon incompatible interpretations;
* the difference is constitutionally justified by distinct missions.

No architectural inconsistency may remain unresolved solely because both offices were previously certified.

# Consistency Principle

Two offices are constitutionally consistent when they apply the same enterprise principle in materially equivalent ways unless a mission-specific distinction has been explicitly justified.

Differences shall be presumed inconsistent when they arise from:

* historical drafting variance;
* implementation convenience;
* office-specific terminology masking identical concepts;
* undocumented exceptions;
* inconsistent certification standards;
* inherited assumptions;
* duplicated doctrine;
* conflicting interpretations.

A difference shall be accepted only when its constitutional necessity is demonstrated.

# Comparison Procedure

For every applicable enterprise principle, the review shall:

1. identify the authoritative enterprise doctrine;
2. identify the Performance Truth interpretation;
3. identify the interpretation used by every compared office;
4. compare requirements, evidence, lifecycle, and failure behavior;
5. identify all differences;
6. determine whether each difference is mission-essential;
7. construct adversarial cross-office scenarios;
8. determine whether the difference can produce conflicting enterprise behavior;
9. classify the difference;
10. issue a resolution or required constitutional amendment.

No comparison may rely solely upon document titles or summary descriptions.

# Difference Classification

Every identified architectural difference shall receive exactly one classification.

## Constitutionally Equivalent

The offices use different language or structure but preserve materially identical guarantees.

No modification is required.

---

## Mission-Justified Specialization

The difference is necessary because the offices govern materially different truth, authority, lifecycle, or operational responsibilities.

The justification shall be explicit and preserved.

---

## Standardization Opportunity

The difference is constitutionally safe but creates unnecessary terminology, evidence, interface, or audit variation.

Standardization should occur before architecture closure.

---

## Architectural Inconsistency

The offices apply shared enterprise doctrine incompatibly.

A constitutional amendment is required.

---

## Enterprise Doctrine Gap

No authoritative enterprise rule determines which architecture is correct.

Enterprise doctrine shall be amended before the inconsistency is resolved.

---

## Certification Inconsistency

The offices were certified against materially different standards for equivalent constitutional properties.

Affected certification shall undergo impact review.

---

## Critical Constitutional Conflict

The difference permits competing ownership, authority, truth, lifecycle control, certification, or unsafe operation.

Affected behavior shall fail closed immediately.

# Cross-Office Challenge Scenarios

The review shall test scenarios including:

* one object referenced by multiple offices under different ownership rules;
* one workflow transitioning between offices with inconsistent lifecycle semantics;
* duplicate messages treated differently by producer and consumer;
* late-arriving correction crossing multiple truth offices;
* evidence accepted by one office and rejected by another;
* conflicting timestamps across office boundaries;
* reconciliation initiated by multiple offices;
* bridge transformation interpreted differently by connected offices;
* one office failing open while another expects fail-closed behavior;
* one office revising truth while another treats the prior version as immutable current truth;
* one office receiving certification despite evidence that would fail another office;
* post-freeze modifications propagated across offices using incompatible rules.

Each scenario shall produce one deterministic enterprise outcome.

---

# Inconsistency Resolution Authority

Where Performance Truth conflicts with another office, the review shall not presume either office correct.

Resolution shall follow this hierarchy:

1. ARGOS Constitution;
2. authoritative enterprise doctrine;
3. explicit cross-office constitutional contract;
4. office-specific doctrine;
5. certified implementation behavior.

Lower authority shall not override higher authority.

Where higher authority is incomplete, an Enterprise Doctrine Gap shall be declared.

---

---

# Finding Severity

## Critical

An inconsistency permits competing ownership, authority, truth, lifecycle control, certification, or unsafe enterprise operation.

Affected behavior shall halt immediately.

---

## Major

An inconsistency materially weakens determinism, traceability, evidence, reconciliation, fail-closed behavior, or auditability.

Architecture closure shall not proceed.

---

## Minor

A safe but unnecessary inconsistency creates avoidable complexity or future ambiguity.

Standardization is required before closure unless formally deferred.

---

## Observation

A non-material architectural difference with no constitutional consequence.

# Fail-Closed Behavior

The Performance Truth Office and every affected enterprise interface shall fail closed whenever:

* cross-office ownership is inconsistent;
* lifecycle authority conflicts;
* interfaces rely upon incompatible guarantees;
* truth hierarchy is disputed;
* reconciliation authority overlaps;
* evidence standards materially conflict;
* temporal semantics can produce different outcomes;
* one office expects behavior another office does not constitutionally guarantee;
* fail-closed behavior is incompatible across a boundary;
* certification standards differ for equivalent properties;
* an Enterprise Doctrine Gap affects active constitutional behavior;
* historical truth may have been produced under conflicting architectural rules;
* a Critical or Major inconsistency remains unresolved.

No affected Performance Truth object shall be created, published, revised, reconciled as complete, certified, or used as authoritative enterprise truth until consistency is restored.

---

# Completion Decision

This order shall produce exactly one completion decision.

## PASS

Performance Truth has been compared against every applicable certified ARGOS office.

All shared constitutional doctrines are applied consistently.

All remaining differences are constitutionally equivalent or mission-justified.

No unresolved Critical or Major architectural inconsistency remains.

---

## CONDITIONAL PASS

No inconsistency threatens current constitutional safety, but bounded Minor standardization findings remain.

Each finding shall possess an approved owner, amendment, and completion condition before Constitutional Architecture Closure.

---

## FAIL

One or more offices apply shared enterprise doctrine incompatibly in a manner affecting ownership, authority, lifecycle, truth, evidence, reconciliation, fail-closed behavior, audit, or certification.

Affected behavior and architecture closure shall remain suspended until amendment and independent re-certification are complete.
