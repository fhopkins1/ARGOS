# ECS-003-LIB-006

# Proof Skeleton Generator

## Doctrine Statement

### Purpose

This work order establishes the authoritative Proof Skeleton Generator governing deterministic creation, identity assignment, structural validation, lineage preparation, coverage reconciliation, and publication of requirement-level proof skeletons under the Enterprise Constitutional Standard (ECS-003).

The Generator shall transform one authoritative canonical constitutional requirement registry into one complete, internally consistent, machine-readable proof skeleton population.

The Generator shall create the required proof structure for every canonical constitutional requirement before execution evidence is attached.

Every proof skeleton shall define the complete lineage required to demonstrate:

```text
Constitutional Authority
→ Canonical Requirement
→ Implementation Obligation
→ Implementation Artifact
→ Behavioral Verifier
→ Behavioral Execution
→ Raw Evidence
→ Normalized Evidence
→ Behavioral Finding
→ Implementation Disposition
→ Proof Object
→ Certification Disposition
```

The Generator shall establish proof structure without fabricating execution, evidence, findings, dispositions, coverage, or certification conclusions.

The canonical requirement registry established under **ECS-003-LIB-005** shall be accepted as the authoritative requirement baseline.

The requirement registry shall remain immutable during proof skeleton generation.

Proof skeleton generation shall create structural proof obligations without altering, weakening, broadening, narrowing, interpreting away, or inventing constitutional requirements.

Every generated proof skeleton shall preserve deterministic lineage to:

* its canonical constitutional requirement;
* its governing constitutional authority;
* its implementation obligation;
* its required verification classes;
* its required evidence classes;
* its required proof fields;
* its generation execution;
* its published proof skeleton record.

No completed proof object shall be asserted merely because a proof skeleton exists.

No missing execution evidence shall be replaced by placeholders that imply successful verification.

No unknown implementation mapping shall be silently inferred.

No absent verifier shall be silently invented.

No missing evidence shall be represented as satisfied.

No unresolved proof obligation shall receive a proven disposition.

---

# Constitutional Principles

Every canonical constitutional requirement shall possess exactly one authoritative proof skeleton.

Every proof skeleton shall possess one deterministic identity.

Every proof skeleton shall possess one canonical requirement identity.

Every proof skeleton shall preserve complete requirement lineage.

Every proof skeleton shall define all execution, evidence, implementation, traceability, and certification obligations required for proof completion.

Proof structure shall be deterministic.

Proof identity generation shall be deterministic.

Proof obligation generation shall be deterministic.

Equivalent normalized requirement registries shall produce semantically equivalent proof skeleton populations.

Proof skeletons shall remain incomplete until objective executable evidence satisfies every mandatory proof obligation.

A proof skeleton shall never constitute proof by itself.

A requirement shall never be classified as proven solely because:

* a skeleton exists;
* an implementation artifact is named;
* a verifier is declared;
* a test name exists;
* a completion report asserts success;
* metadata indicates execution;
* prior certification status exists.

Proof completion shall fail closed.

Missing implementation mapping shall prevent proof completion.

Missing verifier mapping shall prevent proof completion.

Missing execution evidence shall prevent proof completion.

Missing evidence integrity shall prevent proof completion.

Missing execution lineage shall prevent proof completion.

Broken traceability shall prevent proof completion.

Historical proof skeletons shall never be overwritten.

Superseded proof skeletons shall remain preserved.

Proof corrections shall occur through explicit versioning and supersession.

## 2. Canonical Proof Skeleton Identity Generation

Assign exactly one deterministic proof skeleton identity to every active canonical constitutional requirement.

Proof skeleton identities shall be:

* deterministic;
* unique;
* stable;
* reproducible;
* collision-resistant;
* semantically linked to the requirement identity;
* independent of workstation state;
* independent of generation order where feasible;
* independent of wall-clock time except as non-semantic metadata.

Identity generation shall consider:

* constitutional corpus identity;
* canonical requirement identity;
* requirement version;
* proof schema version;
* proof obligation class.

A proof skeleton identity shall not change solely because of:

* formatting;
* whitespace;
* pagination;
* file location;
* output ordering;
* generation timestamp;
* non-semantic metadata.

Semantic changes to the governing requirement or proof obligations shall produce a new proof skeleton version or superseding identity according to explicit supersession rules.

## 11. Applicability Reconciliation

Reconcile proof skeleton generation with requirement applicability.

For requirements classified as `APPLICABLE`:

* generate a complete active proof skeleton.

For requirements classified as `CONDITIONALLY_APPLICABLE`:

* generate a conditional proof skeleton;
* define the activation condition;
* define the evidence required to determine activation.

For requirements classified as `NOT_APPLICABLE`:

* generate an applicability proof skeleton;
* require objective constitutional justification;
* prohibit implementation or behavioral proof requirements unless applicability changes.

No requirement shall be omitted solely because it is classified as `NOT_APPLICABLE`.
