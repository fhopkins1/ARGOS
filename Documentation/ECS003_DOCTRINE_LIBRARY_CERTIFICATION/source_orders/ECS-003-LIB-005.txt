# ECS-003-LIB-005

# Requirement Registry Generator

## Doctrine Statement

### Purpose

This work order establishes the authoritative Requirement Registry Generator governing deterministic extraction, normalization, identity assignment, classification, reconciliation, and publication of canonical constitutional requirements under the Enterprise Constitutional Standard (ECS-003).

The Generator shall transform one authoritative constitutional doctrine package into one complete, atomic, internally consistent, machine-readable canonical requirement registry.

The Generator shall identify every normative constitutional obligation governing:

* constitutional authority;
* office purpose;
* ownership;
* custody;
* prohibited authority;
* canonical objects;
* lifecycle behavior;
* state transitions;
* interfaces;
* dependencies;
* temporal behavior;
* freshness;
* ordering;
* duplicate handling;
* replay;
* recovery;
* reconciliation;
* correction;
* supersession;
* evidence;
* implementation;
* behavioral verification;
* proof;
* traceability;
* reproducibility;
* certification;
* fail-closed behavior.

The constitutional doctrine package shall remain immutable during requirement generation.

Requirement generation shall extract and structure constitutional meaning without altering, expanding, weakening, interpreting away, or inventing constitutional obligations.

Every generated requirement shall preserve deterministic lineage to:

* its constitutional source artifact;
* its source section;
* its source clause;
* its governing authority;
* its extraction rule;
* its normalization rule;
* its generation execution;
* its canonical registry entry.

No canonical requirement shall be created through unrestricted prose synthesis.

No normative constitutional clause shall be silently omitted.

No non-normative statement shall be promoted into a constitutional requirement without objective classification evidence.

No ambiguous constitutional clause shall be silently resolved.

No contradictory constitutional clause shall be silently reconciled.

---

# Constitutional Principles

Canonical constitutional requirements shall originate exclusively from authoritative constitutional doctrine.

Every canonical requirement shall possess one deterministic identity.

Every canonical requirement shall possess one authoritative source.

Every canonical requirement shall express one atomic constitutional obligation.

Every canonical requirement shall possess one constitutional owner.

Every canonical requirement shall possess one obligation subject.

Every canonical requirement shall possess one obligation action.

Every canonical requirement shall possess one obligation object or governed condition.

Every canonical requirement shall possess one applicability disposition.

Every canonical requirement shall possess one verification disposition.

Every canonical requirement shall preserve complete source lineage.

Requirement extraction shall be deterministic.

Requirement normalization shall be deterministic.

Requirement identity generation shall be deterministic.

Equivalent normalized doctrine shall produce semantically equivalent requirement registries.

Requirement generation shall fail closed when doctrine is:

* missing;
* ambiguous;
* contradictory;
* structurally invalid;
* unsupported;
* incompletely referenced;
* incapable of atomic decomposition without interpretation.

No documentation, implementation behavior, source code, test result, completion report, metadata, or historical certification status shall create constitutional authority.

Historical requirement registries shall never be overwritten.

Superseded requirement identities shall remain preserved.

Requirement correction shall occur through explicit supersession.

---

# Objective

Implement one deterministic engine capable of accepting an authoritative constitutional doctrine package and generating one complete canonical constitutional requirement registry suitable for:

* implementation obligation generation;

* behavioral coverage generation;
* verifier mapping;
* evidence generation;
* proof generation;
* execution-derived traceability;
* certification blocker evaluation;
* clean-room reproduction;
* fail-closed certification validation.

# Required Activities

## 1. Authoritative Doctrine Source Validation

Validate the complete constitutional doctrine package before requirement extraction.

Verify:

* doctrine package identity;
* doctrine package integrity;
* artifact completeness;
* artifact authority;
* artifact status;
* artifact precedence;
* artifact version;
* artifact supersession;
* source cross-references;
* defined terminology;
* structural validity.

Classify every source artifact as:

* AUTHORITATIVE_ACTIVE;
* AUTHORITATIVE_SUPERSEDED;
* SUPPORTING_NON_NORMATIVE;
* DRAFT;
* INVALID;
* UNRESOLVED.

Only `AUTHORITATIVE_ACTIVE` artifacts may generate active constitutional requirements.

Superseded artifacts shall remain available exclusively for historical lineage.

---

## 2. Normative Clause Discovery

Identify every potentially normative clause through deterministic structural and semantic rules.

Discover normative clauses expressed through terms including, where constitutionally applicable:

* shall;
* shall not;
* must;
* must not;
* may only;
* is required to;
* is prohibited from;
* is authorized to;
* exists solely to;
* completes only when;
* fails when;
* permits;
* prohibits;
* requires;
* owns;
* governs;
* preserves.

Normative discovery shall not rely solely upon keywords.

The Generator shall also evaluate:

* clause context;
* heading context;
* defined terms;
* exceptions;
* scope;
* cross-referenced obligations;
* completion criteria;
* constitutional principles;
* prohibited actions;
* authority declarations.

Every discovered clause shall receive one classification:

* NORMATIVE_REQUIREMENT;
* NORMATIVE_PROHIBITION;
* NORMATIVE_PERMISSION;
* NORMATIVE_DEFINITION;
* NORMATIVE_COMPLETION_CONDITION;
* NORMATIVE_FAILURE_CONDITION;
* NON_NORMATIVE_CONTEXT;
* AMBIGUOUS;
* CONTRADICTORY;
* DUPLICATE_CANDIDATE.

No `AMBIGUOUS` or `CONTRADICTORY` clause shall be silently converted into an active requirement.

---

## 3. Atomic Requirement Decomposition

Decompose every normative clause into atomic constitutional obligations.

A requirement shall be atomic only when it expresses one independently verifiable obligation.

Split clauses containing multiple independently verifiable obligations.

Preserve combined clauses only when separation would alter constitutional meaning.

For every candidate requirement identify:

* obligation subject;
* normative operator;
* obligation action;
* governed object;
* triggering condition;
* scope;
* constraint;
* exception;
* required outcome;
* prohibited outcome;
* evidence implication;
* verification implication.

Identify:

* aggregate requirements;
* compound requirements;
* nested requirements;
* conditional requirements;
* exception-dependent requirements;
* cross-reference-dependent requirements.

Every decomposition decision shall preserve source lineage.

---

## 4. Requirement Normalization

Normalize each atomic requirement into one canonical structured representation.

Normalization shall preserve constitutional meaning while standardizing:

* terminology;
* defined names;
* normative operators;
* singular and plural forms;
* reference syntax;
* conditional structure;
* exception structure;
* scope representation;
* identity formatting.

Normalization shall not:

* add authority;
* remove restrictions;
* broaden applicability;
* narrow applicability;
* resolve ambiguity through inference;
* replace constitutional terminology with implementation terminology.

For every normalized requirement preserve:

* exact source text;
* normalized text;
* structured representation;
* normalization rule;
* semantic equivalence disposition.

Classify normalization as:

* EXACT_STRUCTURAL_NORMALIZATION;
* AUTHORIZED_TERMINOLOGY_NORMALIZATION;
* AUTHORIZED_ATOMIC_DECOMPOSITION;
* NON_SEMANTIC_FORMATTING_CHANGE;
* SEMANTIC_DIVERGENCE;
* UNRESOLVED.

Any `SEMANTIC_DIVERGENCE` or `UNRESOLVED` disposition shall block publication of the affected requirement.

---

## 5. Canonical Requirement Identity Generation

Assign one deterministic canonical identity to every active atomic constitutional requirement.

Requirement identities shall be:

* deterministic;
* unique;
* stable;
* reproducible;
* collision-resistant;
* independent of workstation state;
* independent of generation order where feasible;
* independent of wall-clock time except as non-semantic metadata.

Identity generation shall consider:

* constitutional corpus identity;
* governing domain;
* source artifact identity;
* semantic requirement identity;
* source lineage;
* atomic requirement content.

A requirement identity shall not change solely because of:

* formatting;
* whitespace;
* pagination;
* file location;
* non-semantic heading changes;
* generated timestamps.

Semantic requirement changes shall generate a new requirement version or identity according to the supersession rules.

## 9. Duplicate and Overlap Detection

Identify requirements with:

* identical normalized meaning;
* overlapping meaning;
* conflicting meaning;
* narrower or broader scope;
* shared source;
* duplicate source clauses;
* superseded meaning.

Classify relationships as:

* EXACT_DUPLICATE;
* SEMANTIC_DUPLICATE;
* PARTIAL_OVERLAP;
* SPECIALIZATION;
* GENERALIZATION;
* CONFLICT;
* INDEPENDENT.

Exact and semantic duplicates shall be reconciled into one authoritative active requirement while preserving all source references.

Specializations and generalizations shall remain separate only where independently verifiable and constitutionally justified.

Conflicts shall block registry release.

---

* constitutional authority;

* exceptions;

* unresolved references;
* circular references;
* missing referenced clauses;
* references to superseded doctrine;
* ambiguous reference scope.

No requirement dependent upon an unresolved reference shall receive active release status.

* NON_SEMANTIC;
* CLARIFICATION;
* SCOPE_REFINEMENT;
* AUTHORITY_CHANGE;
* OWNERSHIP_CHANGE;
* BEHAVIORAL_CHANGE;
* EVIDENCE_CHANGE;
* CERTIFICATION_CHANGE;
* DELETION;
* REPLACEMENT.

Preserve:

* prior requirement identity;
* prior requirement version;
* supersession reason;
* superseding requirement identity;
* effective date;
* source doctrine change;
* historical lineage.

Superseded requirements shall never be deleted from historical registries.

# Fail-Closed Rules

The Generator shall refuse registry release when:

* the doctrine package is invalid;
* an authoritative artifact is missing;
* source precedence is unresolved;
* a normative clause remains unclassified;
* a normative clause remains ambiguous;
* a normative conflict remains unresolved;
* an active requirement is not atomic;
* a requirement identity collision exists;
* a requirement lacks authoritative source lineage;
* a requirement lacks constitutional authority;
* a requirement lacks constitutional ownership;
* a requirement lacks applicability disposition;
* a requirement lacks verification disposition;
* a cross-reference remains unresolved;
* an active duplicate remains unresolved;
* an active requirement conflict remains unresolved;
* a normative source clause lacks requirement coverage;
* an active requirement lacks source coverage;
* deterministic regeneration fails;
* historical supersession lineage is incomplete.

The Generator shall never issue `READY_FOR_RELEASE` merely because a registry file was produced.
